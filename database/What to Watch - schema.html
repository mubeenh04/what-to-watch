What to Watch schema

Eleven tables that back the Home, Explore and Watchlist screens. A title's details live once in the catalogue, where it can be watched lives in availability, and which section it sits in lives in a single status per title, so a card can never show up in two places at once.

Sections and how a title moves

user_title.status is the one place a title's section is stored. The primary key (user_id, title_id) means each title has exactly one status, which is the "one section at a time" rule from your brief. Every move also writes a row to title_status_event, which is what Undo reads.

Movies vs Series tabs

Every page filters on title.title_type. There's no separate table for series in v1.

Home needs a source

A title can only be home while it's on a local drive, or on a service you subscribe to in South Africa (region_code = 'ZA'). Explore only suggests titles with neither. The app (or a trigger) enforces this.

Automatic move to Home

The availability sync reads per-country streaming data (TMDB watch providers, which come from JustWatch). When a Watchlist or Explore title shows up on Netflix South Africa, or another of your services there, it moves to home on its own and is logged as a sync move. Rows for other countries are stored but ignored.

Example: the Home movies tab, local drive only, 2020 or later, IMDb 7.5 and up.

SELECT t.*
FROM   user_title ut
JOIN   title t ON t.title_id = ut.title_id
WHERE  ut.user_id = :me
  AND  ut.status = 'home'
  AND  t.title_type = 'movie'
  AND  t.release_year BETWEEN 2020 AND 2026
  AND  t.imdb_rating >= 7.5
  AND  EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM title_availability a
               WHERE a.title_id = t.title_id AND a.source_type = 'local')
ORDER BY t.imdb_rating DESC;  -- add EXISTS on title_genre for a genre filter

Table definitions

PostgreSQL types for Supabase. The script also turns on row-level security, so each person only sees their own lists and the catalogue is read-only from the app. The full script is saved next to this page as what-to-watch-schema.sql. Sizes are starting points and easy to change.

Assumptions to confirm

One household, room for profiles

I assumed one shared list. Status still hangs off app_user, which is tied to a Supabase sign-in, so adding separate profiles later is a new sign-in, not a redesign.

Series are a single title

No seasons or episodes. Watched means the whole series. If you want to track seasons, a season table under title slots in cleanly.

A title can be in several places

Dune could be on Max and on your drive. Availability allows many rows. The card shows local first, then the service with the lowest display_order.

Scores are cached

IMDb and Rotten Tomatoes scores are copied in from a data source and refreshed on a schedule. Either can be empty when a title has no score yet.

Letterboxd links

Stored per title when known, otherwise the app builds a Letterboxd search link. Letterboxd covers films well but few series.

Nothing is deleted

Removed titles keep their row forever so the block list holds. Hidden is reversible.

Decided: hosted on Supabase

User ids are Supabase Auth UUIDs, and row-level security policies are in the script.

Decided: region-aware auto move

Streaming availability is stored per country. Only South Africa rows on services you subscribe to can move a title to Home automatically.
